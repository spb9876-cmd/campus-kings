Down thirty-one to seventeen. At the half. In the National Championship.

If you tuned into Tampa expecting a coronation, you got a car crash instead, and it's a better story for it. **Ole Miss 35, Oregon 34.** Pat is your Season Three national champion. Write that down, because it has never been written before with any name on it other than Stew's.

## The Turn

Raymond James Stadium had Oregon up 31-17 at the break, and if the game had ended right there, nobody argues with it — Bowzer had just come off the single best win of his life, a comeback from fifteen down to end Stew's three-peat, and he was playing like a man who wasn't done. Fourteen down at halftime of a title game against a defense that had carried Ole Miss all November is supposed to be the part of the story where the door closes.

Pat kicked it back open.

Freshman **Deshaun Oden** — 11 of 19, 272 yards, 2 touchdowns, 2 interceptions, exactly the turnover-marred line you'd expect from a young quarterback asked to erase a two-score deficit on the sport's biggest stage — found **Caleb Cunningham** for four catches, 118 yards and two scores, and the two of them combined to drag the second half into the kind of back-and-forth, turnover-heavy scrap the whole league is still replaying. Neither side let go of the rope. When Ole Miss finally got it, they got it by one point, and not one snap earlier.

**Ole Miss 35, Oregon 34.** First ring in the accredited cycle for both men. Only one of them leaves with it.

## The Symmetry Nobody Asked For But Everybody Noticed

Forty-eight hours earlier, Bowzer beat Stew by scoring twenty-three unanswered points after trailing 15-0 at the half — the single most complete second-half performance anybody has produced against the two-time champ. Tampa handed him the same math with the sign flipped: up fourteen at the break, outscored the rest of the way, gone by one.

Reach the mountain by erasing a fifteen-point hole. Lose it to a seventeen-point one going the other way. That's not bad luck. That's the bracket being exactly as cruel to the good guy as it was kind to him a round earlier, and there isn't a coach in this league who'd tell you Bowzer didn't deserve better than the way it ended.

## What It Means

Let's be precise, because the league has heard this before and it's still true: **the belt did not change hands Saturday.** The Campus King Belt goes to whoever holds the most national championships when all twenty seasons are done, and that's a twenty-season argument, not a one-night one.

What did change: **Pat is a national champion now.** The first name other than Stew's on the championship page in the accredited era. The title race moves to **Stew 2, Pat 1**, with seventeen seasons still to be played. It is close enough, this early, to matter for real — and Pat didn't just win a bracket to get here. He beat Stew twice this season with something on the line before the bracket ever started: 49-10 in the Week 9 Game of the Week, then 33-26 in the SEC Championship, the first time in this league's history anybody has beaten Stew twice in the same season. Add a ring to that and you have a résumé that didn't exist eight weeks ago.

Bowzer walks away with no ring but the signature stretch of anybody's season — the highlight-reel comeback over the two-time champ, then a shootout loss in the title game that he was one defensive stand away from winning. Stew, for his part, is still the only two-time champion in the accredited cycle, and that number still means something. It just doesn't mean No. 1 anymore. See the rankings for why.

Seventeen seasons left. The road just got a lot more interesting.
