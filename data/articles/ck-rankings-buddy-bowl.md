First board of Season Six, all twenty-two chairs. The Atlanta Edition ended with the king alone on the belt and alone at No. 1. Five weeks later he's lost a game, the champ is undefeated with the wrong quarterback, and a rookie is 2-0 with a scalp. The chair moves.

## No. 1 — Pat, Ole Miss (2-0 played, 3-0 on the log)

Beat last year's finalist 17-7 in South Bend with the backup QB by accident, beat last year's 13-0 man 31-21, and collected a forfeit when Texas didn't show. The two-time champ is the only coach on this board with two wins over top-five résumés, and he's done it while "forgetting I had the backup starting." The committee's eleven seed was the funniest decision in league history and this is the receipt.

## No. 2 — Dre, Michigan (2-0 played)

The Buddy Bowl: 41-31 over the 15-0 champion, Oklahoma's first played loss in seventeen games. Plus a 32-31 opener over Texas. The vault allowed 31 and won anyway, which is a new trick. The asterisk is the Week 4 forfeit to TCU — the alarm clock strikes again — and it's the only reason this isn't No. 1.

## No. 3 — Stew, Oklahoma (2-1)

58-22 over States and 35-0 over KJ are the two most violent wins of the young season, and the one loss came on the road to the league's best defense. Three rings, the belt lead, 69 points, and a 2-1 that looks better than most 3-0s. Norman is fine. Norman is always fine. But the chair moved, and the file says why.

## No. 4 — TBO, Arizona State (2-0)

The Cinderella engine is still running: 38-13 over Lute, 31-6 over an undefeated Lux. Two wins by a combined 50. The quietest coach in the league has been top-five on three consecutive boards and nobody has gotten used to it yet.

## No. 5 — Roy, Nebraska (2-0)

Best debut since Norman. Took JT's old team, beat JT's new team 31-28 in a game JT quit as the winning kick was being lined up, then handled Quan's Florida 42-21. Two weeks, two scalps, zero quotes. The board loves a rookie who lets the Sheet talk.

## No. 6 — Curt, Ohio State (2-0)

An overtime thriller over Purp's LSU debut, then 30-14 over Bhog. The founder is undefeated, pessimistic (per States), and running the league's hiring desk, broadcast booth, and rulebook simultaneously. "Cash Rules Everything Around Me!" is the mission statement.

## No. 7 — Bowzer, Oregon (2-0)

Shut out Alabama 28-0, beat a CPU Georgia, and runs the #big-games desk. The B1G runner-up's season hasn't met a real test yet, but the file is clean.

## No. 8 — Lux, Clemson (2-1)

Returned from retirement, beat Tayne by one and Silingi by twenty, then got smoked 31-6 by TBO. Spent the off-hours proposing to the league bot. Four pre-cycle rings, zero that count, and the most entertaining 2-1 on the board.

## No. 9 — Purp, LSU (1-1)

Lost the overtime heartbreaker in Columbus, then put 51 on Miami. The most dangerous out in the field has a new zip code and the same tape.

## No. 10 — Quan, Florida (1-1)

Lost to the rookie 42-21, beat Thrash 42-7. The Gators look like the ACC champion who moved conferences: streaky, loud, dangerous on the right night.

## No. 11 — Zoe, Florida State (1-0)

"FSU Shines in CK debut" — 42-20 over Silingi. One game, one blowout, one very optimistic board placement.

## No. 12 — Tayne, Notre Dame (1-2)

Beat KJ 50-33 in Week 0, lost by one to Lux and by ten to Pat, got his QB hurt on the first play, relitigated a Hulk-play ruling in a full Curt impression, and floated leaving the league mid-argument. The résumé is still a finalist's résumé. The month was not.

## No. 13 — KJ, TCU (0-2 played, 1-0 by forfeit)

Allowed 50 to Tayne and got shut out 35-0 by the king; the only win is Dre's no-show. Back-to-back Big 12 champion, currently 0-2 in games with a human on the other sideline. "we kickin ole miss ass tmr" is scheduled for Week 6.

## No. 14 — States, Penn State (0-2)

58-22 and 31-21. The 13-0 is a year old and the calendar man is in his first real slump. The board remembers he opened last year with the champ's scalp; it also remembers he's lost three straight played games to Ole Miss and Oklahoma since Atlanta.

## No. 15 — JT, Tennessee (0-1)

New team, same opponent, and a 31-28 loss to Nebraska that he didn't finish — "Nebraska was lining up for the GW kick and he quit." The Norman heist feels like a long time ago. The board expects a response; the league expects a fourth quarter.

## No. 16 — Silingi, Texas Tech (0-2 played, 1-0 by forfeit)

## No. 17 — Thrash, South Carolina (0-1)

42-7 to Quan. The Gamecocks' schedule continues to have other plans.

## No. 18 — Bawce, Texas A&M (0-0 played, 1-0 by forfeit)
