Three weeks ago the bracket opened with two unbeatens and the king at the 3-seed. It closed in Glendale with the king holding his fourth ring, the unbeatens holding a combined 1-2 in the games that mattered, and the two-time champ holding a forfeit. **Oklahoma 34, Oregon 13.** Back-to-back. Four of six. The belt reads Stew 4, Pat 2, and the dynasty argument that opened in Miami two years ago has been answered twice in a row by the same man.

## Quarterfinals: the chalk strikes back

**Oklahoma 35, Clemson 0.** Lux's legacy point was followed by a legacy shutout. The wife signed off, the game got played, and the 11-seed that hung 45 on TCU got nothing on the king. Lux's season review, filed the following week: "I beat him a couple times but ck analyst says im a bitch."

**Oregon 70, Nebraska 14.** Bowzer put seventy on the rookie — the biggest playoff margin since the 77-7 — and Roy's debut season ended the way most rookie seasons end against a 13-0 1-seed. Two scalps, one beating, zero quotes. He'll be back under a new handle and the same résumé.

**Florida State 49, Michigan FW.** Two different games, one paragraph each. Zoe's unbeaten rookie season passed its first real test, 49-38 over Purp's LSU in a shootout the Seminoles never trailed late. And the Cotton Bowl — the vault vs the champ, the Season Five rematch everybody circled — was never played. "Did pat and Dre play," LT asked, hunting for tape. Pat: "Nah it was FW." Dre: "Hell Na I beat that mane ass." Per league law it goes on the ledger as Michigan 41-31 by forfeit and is narrated as nothing, which is also how the champ's title defense ended: two rings, a 12-1 regular season, an SEC final, and a bracket exit without a snap.

## Semifinals

**Oregon 28, Michigan 20.** Bowzer beat Dre for the second time in a month — 51-31 in the B1G final, then this — and reached his second national championship game, three seasons after losing the first one to Pat by a point in Tampa. The #big-games desk, for once, had a rooting interest it could admit to.

**Oklahoma 56, Florida State 14.** The unbeaten rookie met the king and the king was not interested in the storyline. Fifty-six. Zoe's season ends 13-1 with an ACC crown, a quarterfinal win over Purp, and a Stew Crew membership that did not include a non-aggression clause.

## The National Championship: Oklahoma 34, Oregon 13

Two unbeaten résumés entered the bracket. The one that lifted the trophy had a loss on it — the Buddy Bowl, Week 2, 41-31 to Dre — and then won nine straight, the last three by a combined 125-27. Stew's fourth ring of the accredited era, his second straight, and the fourth time in six seasons the final score has had Oklahoma on the left.

The chat held its usual ceremony. KJ: "damn 2 nattys in 2 days." "mane the goat." Zoe: "bro got to stop doing people like this man 🤣🤣🤣." Bowzer, the runner-up, with the line of the night: "All the mfs who beat him have moved on and retired to bigger shit 😂." Stew's acceptance speech, in three parts: "Bigger shit? I'm just a regular dude with 3 jobs …" — "I've lost a lot I'm just not a like able person" — and finally, to everyone: "I love you all @everyone no need for hate or dislike." Lux, converted: "Proud of you man. I'm rooting for you to keep winning." LT cut the 14-minute playoff movie and called it "the road to the crown."

Pat, from the cheap seats: "Jokes on yall I won the natty" — attached to a screenshot from a different game.

## The Season Six story

- **The Buddy Bowl and the response.** Dre beat the king in Week 2. Stew then won twelve straight, including a 76-55 shootout that set the league record, a 24-14 SEC final over Pat, and three playoff games by 35, 42, and 21.
- **The two unbeatens.** Bowzer's Oregon went 13-0, won the B1G by hanging 51 on the vault, beat Dre again in the semis, and lost the final — the first 1-seed to reach the title game since the format started. Zoe's Florida State went 13-0 as a rookie, took the ACC on a forfeit, beat Purp, and ran into Norman.
- **The forfeit era.** The Bot decided a lot: Dre's Week 4 no-show, Lux's ACC final, the champ's Cotton Bowl. Three of the season's biggest games have an asterisk instead of a box score.
- **The exits.** Work (returned, see below), Bhog, Tayne (quit a game, kicked), JT (quit a game, left), and Ice Man's announced last ride, which ended at 1-2. Four programs went dark midseason.
- **The rookies.** Roy went 3-3 with two playoff-bracket scalps; Zoe went 13-1 with a crown; Odini inherited a 99-overall Alabama roster with "one star on the offensive line" and beat Florida with it.
- **The rulebook.** Hulk plays, Wildcat, direct snaps, QB RPOs, and the HB Pass all got banned in-season. Tayne's exit and the RPO debate — "Cnt believe uh mf quit the league cuz he cnt handle some harmless rpo's" — ended with the commissioner banning the RPOs anyway.

**The belt: Stew 4, Pat 2**, six of twenty seasons decided. Playoff Points: Stew 89, Pat 45, LT 33, Bowzer 32. The gap at the top is now the widest it has ever been, and the only coach who has ever beaten the king in January forfeited his way out of the bracket. Season Seven opens Wednesday. The king has three jobs and four rings.
