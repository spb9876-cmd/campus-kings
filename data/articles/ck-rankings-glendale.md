The final board of Season Six, all twenty-two chairs that played it. The Buddy Bowl Edition opened the season with the champ on top and the king in third after one loss. The king responded with twelve straight wins and a fourth ring. The board has stopped pretending there's a debate.

## No. 1 — Stew, Oklahoma (10-1)

Back-to-back. Four rings in six seasons. The SEC final over Pat, the shootout record, 35-0 and 56-14 and 34-13 through the bracket, 89 Playoff Points to the next man's 45. The one loss, the Buddy Bowl, is the only thing on the résumé that isn't a crown, and it's the thing that started the run. "I'm just a regular dude with 3 jobs," he said, holding the trophy. The regular dude is the best coach this league has had and it isn't close anymore.

## No. 2 — Bowzer, Oregon (6-1)

13-0 in-game, the B1G crown by twenty over the vault, 70 on the rookie, a semifinal win over Dre, and a second national-title appearance. He lost the final by three scores to the king, which is what everybody does. The best season of his career ends one game short, again — Tampa was a point, Glendale was twenty-one — and the #big-games desk is now the most credible contender outside Norman.

## No. 3 — Dre, Michigan (5-2 played)

The only coach to beat Stew all year. The Game for a third straight season, a Final Four, 59 and 56 on the bottom of the B1G. The two marks against him: a Week 4 forfeit, and a Cotton Bowl he won without playing. Both losses were to Bowzer. The vault is elite; the attendance is a storyline.

## No. 4 — Zoe, Florida State (3-1 played)

A 13-0 rookie season, an ACC crown (by forfeit, counted), a quarterfinal win over Purp, and a 56-14 semifinal lesson from the king. The best debut in league history by any measure that isn't named Norman. The Stew Crew membership did not protect her in January, which is the point of the Stew Crew.

## No. 5 — Pat, Ole Miss (3-1 played)

A 12-1 in-game season, wins over Tayne, States, and Odini by a combined 111-49, the SEC final against the king — and then a Cotton Bowl forfeit that ended the title defense without a snap. Two rings keep him here. The forfeit keeps him from fourth. The belt gap is four to two and the board notes, without pleasure, that the only January win over Stew in league history belongs to a coach who didn't show up for his quarterfinal.

## No. 6 — Purp, LSU (3-2)

Avenged the Week 1 OT loss on Curt in the first round, then lost a 49-38 shootout to the unbeaten rookie. A 31-30 win over Lute, 51 on Miami, and a Death Valley debut season that ended one round short of the usual. The most dangerous out in the field is still the most dangerous out; this year the field had two unbeatens and a king.

## No. 7 — Lux, Clemson (3-3)

Beat Tayne, Silingi, and — the one that mattered — the three-time Big 12 champion, 45-31, in the first round as an 11-seed. Then 35-0 to the king, with a forfeited ACC final on the ledger behind it. "I beat him a couple times but ck analyst says im a bitch." The Analyst has reviewed the file and finds the first half accurate.

## No. 8 — Roy, Nebraska (3-3)

The rookie beat JT's new team in the game JT quit, beat Quan, beat TBO in the first round, and then met Oregon in the quarterfinal and gave up seventy. Two bracket scalps in a debut season and a loss by one to States along the way. Next year he's listed under a different handle and the same ceiling.

## No. 9 — Curt, Ohio State (3-2)

11-1 in-game, the OT win over Purp, the GOTW over States — and a 7-seed that lost The Game and then the first-round rematch to Purp. The founder's best regular season in years ended in two weeks. "I just load up and play against you cheesy mfs, THE END" is the closing argument.

## No. 10 — KJ, TCU (3-3)

Three straight Big 12 crowns, by four over TBO this time. Three straight playoff exits, this time in the first round to an 11-seed, after "I handed the game away." The conference is his. January is not.

## No. 11 — TBO, Arizona State (2-3)

The Cinderella engine finally stalled: two losses to KJ, including the conference final, and a four-point first-round loss to the rookie. Still the quietest top-half coach in the league; the file just didn't have the November it had last year.

## No. 12 — Lute, Texas (2-5)

Two programs, seven played games, two wins — both at Texas, over Tayne and Thrash. The roster audit was public ("it's just the WRs and RBs which is 95% of the offense") and the 31-30 loss to Purp was the closest he came to a signature. Austin has a full offseason now.

## No. 13 — States, Penn State (1-3 played)

A year after 13-0: losses of 36, 10, 6, and a forfeit, one win by a point over the rookie. The calendar man says he's "busy until August of 2028" at the new job and "still around." The board files this season under the same heading.

## No. 14 — Quan, Florida (1-3 played)

Beat Thrash, lost to the rookie, the king, Odini, and (by forfeit) Zoe. The Florida move hasn't found the ACC-champion version yet.

## No. 15 — Bawce, Texas A&M (1-1 played)

Beat Odini 47-13 and lost the record-setting 76-55 to the king. One real win, one historic loss, a "Wear and tear is off" complaint on file, and the Aggies' first played victory in two seasons.

## No. 16 — Odini, Alabama (1-2)

Inherited a 99-overall Alabama with "one star on the offensive line," lost his debut 47-13, beat Florida 38-30, then took 63 from the champ. The fix, per the man himself, is next season.

## No. 17 — Ice Man, Georgia (1-2 played)

The announced last ride: a 49-10 over Thrash, losses to the king and Quan, three forfeits on the log. "This my last ride this year that's why I'm not really tripping." Between the hedges, the vibes retire undefeated.

## No. 18 — Silingi, Texas Tech (0-3)

Lost to the rookie, the returner, and the three-time champ, and collected one forfeit. "Who has been kicking my XP?" remains unanswered.

## No. 19 — Thrash, South Carolina (0-3)

Allowed 42, 49, and 28. The schedule won again.

## No. 20 — Teeric, USC (0-2 played)

59 from Dre and 61 from Bowzer. The rebuild has not left the parking lot and the parking lot is on fire.

## No. 21 — LT, FAU (0-0)

No league games at FAU, but the most productive offseason in the league: the hype videos, the 14-minute playoff movie, the job board. The commissioner's video department is 13-0.

## No. 22 — Jason, Memphis (0-0)

No league games at Memphis either. Two seasons, zero played games. The board awaits a snap.

*Final board of Season Six. The belt: Stew 4, Pat 2, fourteen accredited seasons to go. The points: Stew 89, Pat 45, LT 33, Bowzer 32. Season Seven's roster changes land on the next edition.*
