The bracket that opened with three unbeatens closed with one perfect team, and it was the one that had been perfect the longest. **Oklahoma 37, Notre Dame 21** in Atlanta — Stew's fifteenth win in fifteen games, his third ring of the accredited era, and the end of the belt tie. Two seasons after Pat caught him, the king pulled back ahead. Here's how the bracket got there, and who it ate along the way.

## Round One: the champ goes home

**Michigan 26, Ole Miss 21.** The committee seeded the back-to-back champion eleventh, sent him to the Big House, and Dre's vault did what it does — the title defense ended one game in, by five, against a defense that had surrendered 7, 9 and 7 in its last three real games. Two rings stay on the shelf in Oxford; the third will have to wait.

**Notre Dame 49, Miami 28.** Lute's first playoff trip of the accredited era ran into the 70-point Irish and lasted about as long as expected. Tayne, the self-scouted "shitter" of Week 0, was just getting warm.

**Arizona State 16, Oregon 7.** The quietest coach in the league played the quietest game of the round and sent Bowzer home for the second straight postseason. TBO's Cinderella run started here; nobody noticed yet.

## Quarterfinals: two unbeatens, zero mercy

**Arizona State 27, Penn State 24.** The calendar man's perfect season — 13-0, B1G crown, the champ's scalp in Week 0 — ended by three, to the 10-seed, in the Rose Bowl. States had spent an entire year building the answer to last January. The bracket asked a different question.

**Notre Dame 42, Clemson 21.** LT's 13-0, his first crown in the accredited era, his best team ever — and Tayne took it apart by three touchdowns in the Peach. The commissioner's resume still reads zero rings, and this one hurts more than the others because this one was supposed to be different.

**Oklahoma 40, Michigan 30.** The vault finally met an offense it couldn't close. Stew put 40 on Dre, the first time anybody had scored 30 on Michigan all season, and the king's quarter of the bracket — the one that was supposed to hold Stew-Pat IV — had only one holder left.

**Tennessee 38, TCU 24.** Purp over KJ: the man whose whole regular season was three games long beat the two-time Big 12 champion like it was his thirtieth. Back-to-back Big 12 crowns, back-to-back quarterfinal exits for the Frogs.

## Semifinals

**Notre Dame 42, Tennessee 31.** A year ago Purp shut Tayne out 27-7 in the quarterfinal. This time Tayne hung 42 on him and punched the first title-game ticket of his career. His postgame — "That was a great game yesterday brudda 🤝🏾" — got the Purp treatment: "Mane stop tagging me that wasn't no great game the hell u just be chattin."

**Oklahoma 33, Arizona State 26.** The glass slipper fit for three rounds. TBO took the king to the wire, which is three points closer than anybody but Ice Man got all season, and left as the best 10-seed story the league has produced.

## The National Championship: Oklahoma 37, Notre Dame 21

Week 0, this was 26-0. Atlanta was closer, and Tayne — "Week 0 rematch," he noted, loading up — went in with the correct self-assessment: "was i really suppose to win . No.. i was the underdog." He got 21 on the board, which is 21 more than the first meeting, and then in the fourth quarter, down sixteen, made the call: "4th qtr its over wit bro" — "not about to let him run it up." States, broadcasting from the cheap seats: "a lot of yapping just to concede in natty." Curt, the counterweight: "Man stand in your shit." Tayne's verdict on his own season was the honest one: "i fought i lost." And: "now i have 1 finals app credited." He does. After four seasons of January exits, he finally has the line on the resume. He just had to get it against the Campus King guru.

The congratulations came the way they always do in this league. States: "congrats lil shit head." Stew, asked how he does it, deflected to the one man who's ever beaten him when it mattered: "Pat was just best you seen …"

## The Season Five story

- **Stew's perfect season.** 15-0 in-game, 9-0 in played league games, the SEC crown over Purp 34-29, JT's Heisman ("stole my Heisman award last night while I was in the game"), and the trophy. The belt reads **Stew 3, Pat 2**, five of twenty seasons decided. The dynasty argument that opened in Miami got its first real answer.
- **Three unbeatens, one survivor.** Clemson, Penn State, and Oklahoma all entered 13-0. Two went out in the quarterfinals to lower seeds. The one that survived was the one that had already won two of these.
- **Tayne's arrival.** 5-2 in played games, a 70-point regular-season game, the 1-seed's scalp, the Vols' scalp, and a first finals appearance — all in the season he opened by calling himself a shitter to a bot at four in the morning.
- **TBO's run.** Big 12 runner-up by two points, then three playoff wins from the 10-seed, including the one that ended the only perfect B1G season in league history.
- **The Bot's era.** Twenty-plus forfeits logged, one 7-6 oversleep, a BYU–Ohio State front-office war, and the commissioner's immortal doctrine: "If you haven played or started by 7:15pm ET The Bot will decide!"
- **The forfeit playoff team.** Lute made the field as a 9-seed on the strength of three forfeit wins and zero played ones. He lost by 21 to the Irish and remains the most interesting bracket entry in league history.

The offseason wire is already smoking — the End of Season Recap listed seven open programs, and the chat has been trading rumors of coaches changing addresses all weekend. Those become official when the front office says so, and the site will say so when they do. For now: the king has three. Everybody else has work to do.
