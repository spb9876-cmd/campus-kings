The final board of Season Four, and for the first time all year it ranks every program in the league — all twenty-eight chairs, from the throne to the folding chairs by the door. The Answer Edition ended with the rule holding the chair. The Miami Edition begins with the rule out of arguments.

## No. 1 — Pat, Ole Miss (15-1)

Back-to-back. Two rings in two seasons, both earned the long way: this year's bracket ran through the unbeaten Big 12 champ (77-7), the No. 1 seed (39-36), last season's title-game opponent (28-14), and the hottest team in the field (31-10). He went 6-1 in played games, and the one loss set up a revenge tour so thorough it should come with a documentary. The board has run out of qualifiers. The best coach in the league, holding the receipts.

## No. 2 — Purp, Tennessee (14-2)

The most dangerous out in the field is now the second-best résumé in the league: JT, Tayne, and Dre eliminated by a combined 127-45, an SEC runner-up finish, and the only two losses all season coming against No. 1. The giant-slayer tag is retired — giants don't get slain by accident three rounds in a row. Next season he's not sneaking up on anyone, which might finally be the fair fight he's been asking for.

## No. 3 — Stew, Oklahoma

The No. 1 seed, the SEC crown, the Answer win over the champ, the 39-36 Cotton Bowl war that was three points from changing everything — and still two rings, still the career points lead at 49, still half of the belt tie at two apiece. The file has never been thicker. It just has one problem in it, and the problem keeps winning in January.

## No. 4 — Dre, Michigan

A Final Four, the Fiesta win over LT by five, and a postseason defense that allowed 7 and 30 before Purp detonated it. The 24-0 shutout of Curt in The Game still anchors the regular season. Quietly assembled the fourth-best season in the league without raising his voice once. "No notes" remains the scouting report.

## No. 5 — Tayne, Notre Dame (12-0*)

The asterisk is the whole story: a perfect regular season, a 2-seed, a bye — and a 27-7 home quarterfinal exit that makes it four straight postseasons ending early. Nobody in the league builds a résumé better. Nobody in the league has less to show for it. The board keeps writing the same paragraph and hating it.

## No. 6 — KJ, TCU (13-1)

Ran the table, won the Big 12 by thirty-one, spent thirteen weeks as the league's feel-good story — and then walked into Oxford and became a unit of measurement. The 77-7 doesn't erase the 13-0; it just staples itself to it forever. The Week 1 rematch he demanded on the spot ("i do wanna play you week 1 next szn tho") is already the most anticipated game of Season Five.

## No. 7 — Bowzer, Oregon

A Big Ten championship, a Final Four, and the misfortune of drawing the one guy who owns him in January — his last two postseason exits are both to Pat, by a combined fifteen. The semifinal concession speech ("Nobody in here can fuck with you") was the most honest scouting report filed all season. Runner-up two years ago, conference champ now; the machine is real.

## No. 8 — LT, Clemson

The 128-28 wrecking-ball stretch, an ACC title-game appearance, and a five-point quarterfinal exit to the eventual Final Four. Three seasons of contender-grade football, zero rings, maximum dignity. The commissioner's program remains the best team nobody wants to see in October and the unluckiest one in December.

## No. 9 — States, Penn State

A 4-seed and a 3-0 record in played games — and a postseason that ended at the office. The B1G title loss and the quarterfinal both went down as forfeits while work "kicked ass," making States the highest-seeded team ever eliminated by a calendar. The played-game file says top five. The availability file says this.

## No. 10 — JT, Nebraska (11 wins)

Rookie of the year, and it's not close: the Norman upset, an 11-win season, a playoff berth, and a first-round education in Neyland. He arrived, he shocked the king, and by his own account he finished the year "on my phone not paying attention." The ceiling is obvious. So is the learning curve.

## No. 11 — Quan, Florida State

An ACC crown in his trophy case and a 27-7 first-round exit to end it. "Relax steward" was the quote of the bracket. A whole career's worth of highs and lows compressed into one season; the crown counts forever.

## No. 12 — Bawce, Texas A&M

Two points from an SEC championship — 23-21 to the eventual No. 1 seed — and out of the field entirely. The thinnest margin between a signature season and a rebuild year anybody experienced. The board suspects he'll be back in that game.

## No. 13 — Work, Texas

Won the games that were winnable — the 42-39 Lone Star thriller over Bawce headlining — and missed the bracket anyway. The quiet 2-1 file of a program one signature win from mattering in December.

## No. 14 — ZBO, SMU

Two real wins and steady all season. The board notes SMU is now the best team in Dallas that didn't lose by seventy.

## No. 15 — Lute, Miami

Split his season 2-2 and then hosted the national championship at his own stadium — the first coach in league history to sell popcorn at somebody else's coronation. Hard Rock was lovely. The Hurricanes were not invited to it.

## No. 16 — TBO, Arizona State

Beat LSU in Week 2 and made the Big 12 title game, where TCU's farewell tour ran him over 45-14. The file says "solid, one round short" in every category.

## No. 17 — Curt, Ohio State

The founder's roughest year on record: 1-4 in user play, shut out in The Game, and reduced to his best role — play-by-play. "National Championship Live in the chat" remains the league's official broadcast signal, and Curt remains its voice, even in a down year. The Buckeyes reload; the OG's handbook endures.

## No. 18 — Ice Man, Georgia

One signature — the GOTW win over Alabama — surrounded by a 1-3 file. Between the hedges, the vibes remain better than the record.

## No. 19 — Silingi, Texas Tech (1-0)

The league's great undefeated technicality: one played game, one 59-35 dismantling of BYU, zero losses that count. The Analyst called his season "a bye week with a scoreboard attached" and got fact-checked within the hour. Smallest file on the board; cleanest one too.

## No. 20 — Thrash, South Carolina

Beat USC, then drew the king and the Aggies back-to-back. Schedule roulette is a cruel game.

## No. 21 — Jason, Alabama

0-2 in played games, but his real contribution to Season Four is in the champ's backfield: Logan McKenna, the freshman Jason "sold the playin style" on and let walk, just ran for 119 and two scores in the national championship. Alabama's best recruiter remains its most generous.

## No. 22 — Harry, Georgia Tech

The standard initiation: 0-4, with a six-point near-miss on SMU. Every legend on this board started somewhere near here. Norman is proof of where rookies can go by November.

## Nos. 23-28 — The open road

**Frank, Washington** and **Teeric, USC** and **Lux, Cal** each logged one and took their lumps; **Gooch, Indiana** (0-2) and **Bhog, BYU** (0-3) fed the contenders; **Cozy, Baylor** logged no played games at all, which the board scores as a perfect defensive season. Four programs — Florida, Auburn, LSU, UCLA — sit dark and open, waiting for Season Five's newcomers to write the next Norman story.

---

*Final board of Season Four. The belt: Stew 2, Pat 2, sixteen accredited seasons to go. The points: Stew 49, Pat 41, LT 29. See you at the draft.*
