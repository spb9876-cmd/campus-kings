Twelve weeks of Season Three are in the books and nothing about the postseason is official yet — no conference title games on the sheet, no seeds, no bracket. But the résumés are real, the records are real, and the picture is clear enough to draw. Here's where the field stands, who's got a bye locked up, who's fighting for a top seed, and what it means for the belt. Everything below is computed off confirmed results through Week 12 — real games only, forfeits and sims excluded.

---

## Bye Watch

Four spots, and right now this is a two-conference conversation.

**Pat (Ole Miss, SEC) — 5-0.** The résumé line of the season is still the 49-10 beatdown of the champ in Week 9, and Pat hasn't blinked since — a 42-20 win over LSU to close the window. Unbeaten, and the only coach in the league with a head-to-head win over Stew. If seeding rewards this season's best case, it starts here.

**Stew (Oklahoma, SEC) — 5-1.** One loss, and it's the loudest one anybody's taken all year. But two rings in two seasons is still two rings in two seasons, and a Week 11 win over Tennessee — survived, not cruised — keeps him right behind Pat. The résumé says he shouldn't be seeded outside the top two anywhere. The record says the man who beat him is currently ranked ahead of him. Both are true at once.

**States (Penn State, B1G) — 5-1.** A Big Ten title and a Final Four appearance on the résumé, and this stretch was the loudest argument yet — 42-20 over Indiana, then 61-14 over USC, the second-largest output by anybody all season not named Ohio State's Week 4 explosion.

**Bowzer (Oregon, B1G) — 5-0.** Quietly perfect, same as always, and nobody's talking about it — again. No losses, no close calls, and a résumé (S2 quarterfinal) that's finally starting to catch up to the record.

That's two SEC coaches and two B1G coaches occupying all four bye spots, which means the two conferences producing the league's best football right now are also squeezing everybody else out of the top four — including a coach who's actually unbeaten.

## In the Field

**Dre (Michigan, B1G) — 4-0.** The best pure winning percentage in the league, and he's still on the outside of the bye tier. Blame the schedule — one fewer game played than the coaches ahead of him — but 4-0 is 4-0, and if Michigan keeps winning, this tier isn't big enough to hold him much longer.

**Lute (Miami, ACC) — 3-0.** The ACC's clear frontrunner, and the cleanest stretch of anybody's season not already mentioned above — capped by a 36-0 shutout of Florida State a few weeks back.

**TBO (Arizona State, BIG12) — 2-1.** The defending Big 12 champion picking up where last year left off. Nobody else in the conference has played enough real football yet to challenge him for the top seed.

**LT (Clemson, ACC) — 1-0.** One game logged all season, and it's almost beside the point — two national championship game appearances is the kind of résumé that buys patience, and buys a seed, even on a thin sample.

**Jason (Alabama, SEC) — 4-3.** Three weeks ago this spot didn't exist. Now it does: 37-13, 49-7, 35-14 — a Game of the Week win over Georgia to close it — and Alabama is playing better football than almost anybody in the league right now, résumé be damned.

**Purp (Tennessee, SEC) — 2-2.** Two playoff appearances on the résumé and a season built on split decisions — he beat Jason 45-42 back in Week 9, then lost the Week 11 Game of the Week to Stew 27-21. Both losses came against exactly the kind of teams a committee wants to see on a schedule.

**KJ (TCU, BIG12) — 1-0.** Silent for most of the season, then a 52-33 shootout win over Baylor in Week 11 to finally get on the board. The best debut season this board has ever tracked isn't ancient history yet.

**Tayne (Notre Dame, IND) — 1-2.** No conference title game path for an independent, which makes every game from here a résumé game. A 63-57 Final Four classic two seasons ago still means something. So does going 1-2 right now.

## The Conference Races

**SEC:** This is Pat and Stew, and everybody in the league knows it. Pat's already beaten him once — 49-10, the worst loss of Stew's Oklahoma tenure — and if these two meet again for the conference, it's the rematch of the year regardless of what else is on the board. Jason's red-hot closing kick and Purp's split-decision résumé are both alive in the background, but neither has the case Pat and Stew do.

**B1G:** A legitimate three-way logjam. Bowzer and Dre are both unbeaten. States has the best résumé and the most conference wins. Nobody has separated, and with Michigan and Ohio State playing their rivalry game as this posts, that picture is about to move.

**BIG12:** TBO's the favorite by default — he's the only coach in the conference who's played more than one real game. KJ's Week 11 breakout keeps TCU in the conversation, but the sample is still thin.

**ACC:** Lute's unbeaten and hasn't been seriously tested. LT's résumé says don't count him out even at 1-0 — two runner-up finishes have a way of making a coach dangerous the moment he gets reps.

## On the Bubble

**Curt (Ohio State, B1G) — 2-2.** Two Final Four trips on the résumé, and right now that's doing more work than the record. Needs a signature win — and The Game against Dre, playing out as this posts, is about as signature as it gets.

**Cell (LSU, SEC) — 1-3.** A Week 10 Game of the Week win over Texas A&M got wiped out by back-to-back blowout losses to Alabama and Ole Miss. Needs to string two wins together before this window closes for good.

**Ice Man (Georgia, SEC) — 3-3.** Beat Florida by 21, then ran straight into the Alabama buzzsaw in the Week 12 Game of the Week. Needs a bounce-back the rest of the way.

**Bawce (Texas A&M, SEC) — 2-2.** The Week 10 Game of the Week loss to LSU stings more now that LSU immediately got run over by Alabama. Needs a real statement win to separate from the pack.

**ZBO (SMU, ACC) — 2-3.** A signature win over Lux's Cal debut isn't the kind of result that moves a résumé. Needs to find one that is.

## The Belt

Stew is bidding for a three-peat, and the résumé board still has him at No. 1 — two rings in two seasons doesn't get erased by one Saturday, however ugly the score. But the bracket doesn't care about résumés. It cares about who's in front of you, and right now the man most likely to stand between Stew and a third ring is the same one who already put 49 on him. If Pat wants to actually take the belt instead of just embarrassing its holder for a week, beating him again — when it counts — is the only way anybody's ever done it in this league. Everybody else chasing a bye or a bid is playing for a shot at getting in the same room as that fight.
