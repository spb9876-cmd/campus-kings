Thirteen weeks are in the books, four conference titles are settled, and the twelve-team bracket is set. Oklahoma finished 13-0 and never really looked threatened. Kewan Lacy won a second straight Heisman. And a CPU-controlled Memphis team is somehow in the field again.

Here's how the regular season closed, how the four title games went, and what the bracket asks of everybody.

---

## Closing Weeks

**Week 12** was quiet by comparison. Cell put 45 on Ice Man's Georgia, Bowzer handled Nebraska 41-14, States got past Washington 31-22, and Teeric beat Gooch 35-14.

**Week 13** was where the seeding got decided, and four teams made statements:

- **Clemson 47, South Carolina 0.** LT shut Thrash out completely to close the regular season. No points, no film worth keeping.
- **Ohio State 54, Michigan 14.** Curt buried Dre by 40 in the rivalry game — and then, a week later, lost the conference title game by four. More on that below.
- **Oregon 41, Washington 3.** Bowzer's second straight 41-point outing, and Frank's second beating in two weeks.
- **Oklahoma 50, Texas A&M 24.** Stew moved to 12-0 with the SEC title game still to come.

Elsewhere: Work dismantled Cell 42-7, Polo took the rivalry game from Quan 33-7, and TV beat Teeric 28-7 in a Los Angeles game that finished USC's month at 1-2.

## The Conference Championships

Two of these were over early. Two went to the wire.

### SEC — Oklahoma 55, Florida 13

Stew wasn't sharing the trophy. Forty-two points of margin in a conference title game, against a Florida team that came in 11-1 and had beaten Alabama and Georgia inside a month. Polo had been the story of the second half of the season; Stew ended that story in one night and finished **13-0**.

### B1G — Penn State 21, Ohio State 17

The best of the four. States and Curt traded a four-point game for the Big Ten, seven days after Curt won the Michigan game by forty. States takes the title and the **3 seed**; Curt takes a **9 seed** and a first-round road trip. Two conference title games decided by 42 and by 4, on the same weekend.

### ACC — Clemson 31, Miami 10

LT's third straight win over Lute, and the widest of them. Clemson finishes 12-1 with the **2 seed** and a bye.

### BIG12 — Arizona State 26, TCU 15

TBO wins a conference championship and still has to play a road game in the first round. Arizona State comes out of it 11-2 but ranked 15th, which lands them at the **11 seed** — the lowest-seeded conference champion in the field, and the clearest illustration of how much the poll weighs the Big 12 right now. KJ's TCU falls at 15.

## The Bracket

**Byes:** Oklahoma (13-0), Clemson (12-1), Penn State (12-1), Oregon (11-1). Stew, LT, States, Bowzer — three conference champions and the Big Ten runner-up that wasn't.

**First round:**

- **12 Memphis (11-2) at 5 Notre Dame (11-1).** Winner draws Oregon in the Rose Bowl. Memphis is CPU-controlled and has now made the field in both accredited seasons, which is either a quirk of the sim or the most consistent program in the league depending on how generous you're feeling.
- **9 Ohio State (10-3) at 8 Ole Miss (10-2).** Curt at Pat, in the Sugar Bowl bracket. Ole Miss has the Heisman winner in the backfield; Ohio State has a 54-14 rivalry win and a four-point heartbreak in the last two weeks. Winner gets undefeated Oklahoma.
- **11 Arizona State (11-2) at 6 Miami (11-2).** TBO at Lute, a conference champion visiting a team that just lost its own title game by 21. Winner gets Penn State in the Fiesta.
- **10 Tennessee (10-2) at 7 Florida (11-2).** Purp at Polo in the Peach bracket. Purp has been the hottest coach in the league since Week 8; Polo has beaten Alabama and Georgia and just been run off the field by Oklahoma. Winner gets Clemson.

It ends in **New Orleans at the Caesars Superdome**.

The bracket math is unkind to the defending champion's half in one specific way: Oklahoma's quarterfinal opponent comes out of Ohio State–Ole Miss, which is the only first-round game between two teams that finished with double-digit losses on nobody's ledger and a combined 20-5 record. The 8/9 line is the strongest in the field.

## Kewan Lacy Goes Back-to-Back

Ole Miss has now produced both Heisman winners in league history, and it's the same player.

**Kewan Lacy: 2,342 rushing yards, 31 total touchdowns, 12.0 yards per carry.** That last number is the one to sit with. Twelve yards a carry over a full season isn't a good year, it's a broken defense simulator. He beat his own precedent and made the vote unanimous well before the ceremony.

The three finalists behind him all have a case they'll never get to make:

- **Emeka Armour** (Florida, HB) — 1,419 yards, **34 touchdowns**, 8.0 per carry. Three more scores than the winner. Nine hundred fewer yards.
- **Quinton Martin Jr.** (Penn State, HB) — 1,803 yards, 24 touchdowns, 10.0 per carry. This is the back who went for 304 and four scores at Autzen in Week 10 and still lost. He'll get another crack at Oregon's half of the bracket.
- **Dezmen Roebuck** (Washington, WR) — 1,660 receiving yards, 21 touchdowns, 115 catches. A hundred and fifteen receptions on a team that lost 41-3 to Oregon and 31-22 to Penn State. Somebody was throwing it.

Lacy's reward for the second trophy is a first-round home game against a Big Ten team that just lost a conference championship by four points. No rest for anybody.
