Forty-eight hours. That's roughly how long it took this league to go from four open conference championships to a completed Final Four with a national title game sitting on the calendar. Four trophies, twelve teams, eight playoff games, and the bracket didn't just move fast — it ran through three separate No. 1 seeds and spit out a championship matchup nobody would've called a week ago.

**Oregon vs. Ole Miss. Tampa. Raymond James Stadium.** Neither man has an accredited-cycle ring. One of them is about to get his first.

---

## The Three Trophies You Missed

We already told you about the SEC — Pat over Stew, 33-26, the first time anybody's beaten him twice in a season. The other three finals landed just as hard.

**Michigan's Dre captured his second Big Ten title, edging Oregon's Bowzer** in a game that read like foreshadowing the moment the bracket came out. **Clemson's LT won his third straight ACC Championship over Miami**, a rematch of last year's final that went the same way twice. And **TCU's KJ beat Arizona State for the Big 12** — the exact rematch of last season's final, except this time KJ got the trophy instead of TBO.

Enjoy that Big 12 crown, KJ, because it bought you exactly one home game.

---

## First Round: The Two-Seed Nobody Saw Coming

Seeded off the full résumé — records, conference hardware, strength of schedule — the bracket set up 1 Michigan and 2 Clemson with byes at the top, 3 Ole Miss and 4 Oregon right behind them. Then round one happened:

**Miami 38, UAB 35** — Lute needed every point to survive the CPU-controlled auto-qualifier that snuck in at 23rd in the country.
**Oklahoma 31, Notre Dame 14** — Stew handles Tayne, business as usual.
**Alabama 38, Penn State 24** — Jason's hot streak keeps rolling; States is out.
**Tennessee 21, TCU 7** — Purp smothers the Big 12 champion. One round, one trophy, done.

That last one is the headline. TCU had just beaten Arizona State for a conference title less than 24 hours earlier. The bracket didn't care.

---

## Quarterfinals: Two Unbeatens Go Home

This is where it got violent. Cotton Bowl, Rose Bowl, Sugar Bowl, Orange Bowl — all four games, one round, and by the time it was over both undefeated teams left standing were gone.

**Oregon knocked out Miami** in the Cotton Bowl, Bowzer closing it out late Thursday night. "Yeah, just used up all my luck for the year — that was your game," he told Lute after. Lute wasn't as generous about the finish: "Gg's," was all he had left in the tank.

**Oklahoma knocked out Michigan** in the Rose Bowl — the undefeated No. 1 seed, gone, at the hands of the same 8-seed script Stew ran all the way to a title in Season One. History repeating on command.

**Ole Miss handled Alabama** in the Sugar Bowl, Pat taking down Jason to keep rolling. Jason's parting shot afterward had the right amount of gallows humor: "the king gonna play one or two more seasons before I retire."

**Tennessee upset Clemson** in the Orange Bowl. Yes, that Clemson — 13-0, three-straight ACC champs, gone. Purp's defense, which just finished off the Big 12 champion a round earlier, did it again to the ACC's.

Final score of the top-seeded, twelve-team field: two 13-0 teams, zero of them left.

---

## Semifinals: The Belt's Whole Story in One Half

**Oregon vs. Oklahoma** was the marquee, and it delivered a gut-check. Stew — the two-time defending champ, the 8-seed making his usual run — had Bowzer down 15-0 at halftime. Bowzer erased it. Whatever happened in that second half, it ended Stew's three-peat bid in the same round it started him toward two rings in Season One. Class was still the last word: **"Gg, proud of you, man,"** Stew wrote to Bowzer when it was over. **"Gg bro,"** came back.

**Ole Miss vs. Tennessee** wasn't as dramatic on the scoreboard but the outcome mattered just as much — Pat kept his run going, sending Purp's improbable three-round sprint (TCU, Clemson, and now this) home one win short of the title game. Even the league group chat needed a beat to process it: **"Pat won the finals? Now?"** Tayne asked, forty-five minutes after the semifinal kicked off.

---

## What's Left

**Oregon (Bowzer) vs. Ole Miss (Pat), Tampa, Raymond James Stadium.** Two coaches who have never held the belt, playing for it in a building neither of them has been to before. Stew's two rings stay the standard until somebody actually takes it from him in the bracket — and this is the first time in the accredited era that "somebody" isn't going to be Stew himself. Whoever wins Tampa gets a first ring and a real argument. Whoever loses becomes this league's newest LT.
