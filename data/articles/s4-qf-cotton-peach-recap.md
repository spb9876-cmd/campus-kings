Two quarterfinals down, and the bracket just delivered the game it had been threatening to deliver since August. Stew-Pat III arrived two rounds early, and across town, the league's most decorated regular-season team found out — again — that November doesn't count for much in January.

## Cotton Bowl: Ole Miss 39, Oklahoma 36

Stew came in 12-1, SEC champion outright over Texas A&M (23-21), and fresh off strolling into Oxford in Week 11 and hanging 38 on Pat, 38-20. By every measure except the one that was about to matter, Oklahoma was the best team left standing — the one seed, the belt-race leader, the coach with two rings already sitting in the case.

Pat had other plans. Coming off the 77-7 record-book demolition of TCU, Ole Miss and Oklahoma played the rubber match nobody in the league chat could stop talking about, and it went the way rubber matches are supposed to go: right down to the wire. "Dline is stupid," Pat reported mid-game. Then the stream started fighting back — "shit laggy asl" — and somehow through the lag, Ole Miss found three more points than Oklahoma did. Final: 39-36. Stew's only other blemish this season came in Week 3, when JT caught him sleeping in Norman, 46-28 — which means the two losses on a 12-2 résumé both came from teams still alive in this bracket.

The GGs were quick and clean. "Gg," Stew offered. "gg," Pat answered back, before turning the postgame into a full media availability. Someone patched the league's Discord analyst bot into the chat, and Pat couldn't resist asking it for a verdict: "What are your thoughts on that Cotton Bowl?" The answer didn't pull punches — "Stew-Pat is 2-1 now across three seasons, and you've stopped being the challenger — you're the standard. Enjoy the belt talk while it lasts, Coach — Stew's still sitting on the ring count, and this league does not do participation trophies." Pat's response: "that shit amazing fym."

That's the real scoreboard here. Stew still leads the twenty-season title race 2-1 — a Week 11 beatdown and a one-seed don't change that, and neither does one quarterfinal loss. What changes is that Pat has now beaten him in a game that mattered twice in three tries, and the gap between "challenger" and "the guy chasing the belt" just got a little smaller.

## Peach Bowl: Tennessee 27, Notre Dame 7

Less drama, same brutal conclusion. Notre Dame finished the Season Four regular season 12-0 — unbeaten, a two-seed, a bye into the quarterfinal — and it bought Tayne exactly one home date before Purp's defense walked in and made it look easy. The chat traffic in the game channel told the story in miniature: Tayne went live at 1:07 AM, and by 1:17 the only word out of him was "lagg." Twenty-seven to seven said the rest.

It is now four playoff appearances, four different flavors of exit, zero rings for Tayne: Michigan bounced him 31-7 in the Season One quarterfinal, Oklahoma sent him home 63-57 in the Season Two semifinal, Oklahoma again in Season Three, first round, 31-14, and now Tennessee in the Season Four quarterfinal. The résumé keeps growing. The trophy case doesn't.

Purp, meanwhile, is quietly building the scariest playoff run in the field — Nebraska by 28 in Round One, an unbeaten team by 20 in the quarterfinal — and he's waiting on the other half of the bracket to sort itself out before he finds out who he's playing for a trip to the title game. It might be Pat. Two coaches, two eliminations of higher seeds, zero fear.
