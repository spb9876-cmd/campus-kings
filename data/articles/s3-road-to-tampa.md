Forty-eight hours. That's roughly how long it took this league to go from four open conference championships to a completed Final Four with a national title game on the calendar. Four trophies, twelve teams, and a bracket that ran through every favorite it met — including both unbeatens.

**Oregon vs. Ole Miss. Tampa. Raymond James Stadium.** Neither man has an accredited-cycle ring. One of them is about to get his first.

## The Three Trophies You Missed

We already told you about the SEC — Pat over Stew, 33-26, the first time anybody's beaten him twice in a season. The other three landed just as hard:

- **B1G: Michigan 40, Oregon 28.** Dre's second Big Ten title, and at the time it read like a coronation for the unbeaten No. 1. Hold that thought.
- **ACC: Clemson 35, Miami 14.** LT's third straight ACC Championship, over the same man he beat for the last one. Hold that thought too.
- **BIG 12: TCU 41, Arizona State 9.** The exact rematch of last season's final, except this time KJ didn't just take the trophy from TBO — he took it by thirty-two.

Enjoy that crown, KJ, because it bought you exactly one home game.

## Quarterfinals: Both Unbeatens Go Home

**Rose Bowl: Oklahoma 35, Michigan 32.** The undefeated, top-seeded, fresh-off-a-conference-title Michigan team — out, by three, to the 8-seed running the exact script that won Stew a ring from the same line in Season One. Dre's perfect season ends at 14-1 without a losing performance in it.

**Cotton Bowl: Oregon 38, Miami 37.** One point. Bowzer's own review, verbatim, to Lute: "Yeah, just used up all my luck for the year — that was your game." Lute's entire response: "Gg's."

**Sugar Bowl: Ole Miss 31, Alabama 28.** The hottest team of the last month took the SEC champ to the wire, and Pat closed. Jason's exit line deserves printing: "the king gonna play one or two more seasons before I retire."

And Tennessee advanced to the semifinals, where Clemson's 13-0 season ends — the second unbeaten gone in a single round.

## Semifinals

**Oregon 23, Oklahoma 15.** Stew had it 15-0 at halftime — vintage postseason Oklahoma, the three-peat forming right on schedule. Bowzer then scored **twenty-three unanswered** and ended the defending champ's season without giving the half back. Class was still the last word in the chat: **"Gg, proud of you, man,"** from Stew. **"Gg bro,"** back from Bowzer. Two rings walk out; the man who runs the league's highlight channel walks on.

**Ole Miss 45, Tennessee 28.** Pat put a firm end to Purp's three-round sprint, one win short of Tampa. The group chat needed a moment to process the bracket's pace — "Pat won the finals? Now?" Tayne asked, mid-round.

## What's Actually at Stake in Tampa

Let's be precise, because this matters: **the belt is not on the line Saturday.** The Campus King Belt goes to one coach — whoever holds the most national championships when all twenty accredited seasons are done. Stew leads that race 2-0, and no result in Tampa changes his count.

What's at stake is a **first ring** — for Bowzer, who just beat the two-time champ from fifteen down, or for Pat, who beat him twice before the bracket ever started. The winner cuts the all-time title race to 2-1 with seventeen seasons still to play, and becomes the first name other than Stew's on the championship page. The belt is a twenty-season war. Tampa is the first real counterattack.
