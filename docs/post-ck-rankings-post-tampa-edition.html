CK Rankings: Post-Tampa Edition

Swept by Pat, beaten in the bracket by Bowzer — and Stew still holds No. 1, because nobody has matched two rings. Pat closes to a coin flip, and Season Four decides how it lands.

Published Aug 30, 2026
Open the full graphic →

He's still No. 1. Let's get that out of the way in the first paragraph, because the whole point of this format is that we don't bury the call.

Stew holds. And understand exactly what that top line just survived: Bowzer beat him in the bracket, erasing nothing less than Stew's own 15-0 halftime lead to end the three-peat. Pat swept him at stakes — 49-10 in the Week 9 Game of the Week, 33-26 for the SEC crown, the first sweep of his accredited life — and then went and won the whole thing. That is the worst eight weeks Stew has ever had in this league. And the math at the top of a board built on ALL seasons still reads the same way it did in August: two rings to one. Nobody else's résumé says "two-time national champion." Until somebody matches that line or takes it from him again, the seat is his. What changed is the distance to it: the gap at the top used to be a canyon. After Tampa, it's a coin flip. But a coin flip means the champ keeps the chair.

Here's the exchange that tells you the pressure at the top was real — before the bracket even opened, in the league chat: "we will have a new champion this year, Oklahoma will be back next season," Stew wrote. Pat's answer: "Heard that bullshit before." Stew, needling back: "Win 3 straight you have to switch." Pat: "So ya been letting me win huh." Stew: "Hell no." Two weeks later Pat had the ring and Stew was watching the final from home. And Pat's own accounting of the season landed harder than anything we could write: "Beat the mf twice an I can't get no help." He didn't get help. He didn't need it. And it still wasn't enough to take the top line — that's how heavy two rings are.

No. 1

Stew — Oklahoma. Still the only two-time champion in the accredited cycle, and on a board built from the whole body of work, that is still the trump card. Yes: swept by Pat this season. Yes: beaten in the bracket by Bowzer from fifteen up at the half. The worst season of his tenure — and his résumé is still the one everybody else is chasing. The chair doesn't change hands on a bad month. It changes hands when somebody matches two rings, and nobody has.

No. 2

Pat — Ole Miss. A ring, an SEC Championship, and a 2-0 season sweep of the two-time champ, capped by an 18-point comeback in the title game itself — down 31-17 at the half in Tampa, won by one. The strongest single season anyone has played in the accredited era, and it moves him to within a coin flip of the top line. One more season like this and the argument ends the other way.

The Bridesmaids

Bowzer — Oregon climbs from mid-pack to the doorstep on the back of the two best halves this league has seen from one coach in a single postseason: twenty-three unanswered to erase 15-0 and end Stew's season, then a shootout loss in Tampa he was one stop away from winning. No ring yet. Nobody's résumé moved further, faster, this window. LT — Clemson holds after a quiet finish — a third straight ACC title, then a 13-0 season that ended in the quarterfinal round without a game actually being played (the note reads FW; we're not holding a forfeit against him, or crediting him one). Two national title-game losses, both to Stew, are still the most painful résumé on the board, and they still count. Dre — Michigan banked a second Big Ten title and a 13-0 run before running into the exact 8-seed script that once made Stew a champion — Oklahoma 35, Michigan 32 in the Rose Bowl, no shame in it. States — Penn State still has the Big Ten title and Final Four from a year ago; this postseason ended in the first round, a real 38-24 loss to Alabama, and the board notes it plainly rather than pretending it didn't happen.

The Climb

Purp — Tennessee is the riser nobody's talking about: smothered the Big 12 champion in the first round, advanced when Clemson's quarterfinal went to forfeit, and made his first Final Four before Ole Miss ended it 45-28. That's a real jump. Tayne — Notre Dame ran into Stew in round one and lost 14-31 — business as usual for the matchup — but the Season Two Final Four run, 140-70 across a bracket, is still the signature résumé line in this tier. Curt — Ohio State missed the bracket outright after a soul-crushing 50-48 four-overtime loss to Michigan in Week 13; a Season One Final Four keeps him here, not any result from this stretch. Lute — Miami turned a first-round survival over the CPU auto-qualifier into a one-point Cotton Bowl classic against Oregon before it slipped away — the kind of quarterfinal loss that reads better than the record shows. Jason — Alabama kept the hot streak going through the first round before Ole Miss shut it down by three in the Sugar Bowl; "the king gonna play one or two more seasons before I retire," he said walking out, and the résumé backs the swagger even in the loss. KJ — TCU finally has the trophy — Big 12 champion, beating Arizona State by thirty-two — before the bracket ended it in round one against Tennessee. First conference title changes the calculus on him permanently. TBO — Arizona State is on the other side of that game: a Big 12 runner-up finish this year after winning it last year, the résumé now reads title-and-runner-up instead of just the rise.

Proving Ground

Work — Texas still owns one of the loudest results in league history — a 52-49 win over Stew in the Season One SEC title game — and closed this season with a 35-14 win over Bawce's Texas A&M. Silingi — Texas Tech has a Big 12 title that's further away every window it doesn't play a meaningful game. Polo — Florida is a former quarterfinalist treading water, closing the year with a loss to Florida State. Ice Man — Georgia has a Season One playoff trip to lean on and not much recent traction — a 35-14 loss to Alabama in Week 12 is the headline. Cell — LSU absorbed two more blowouts this stretch, 49-7 to Alabama and 42-20 to Ole Miss, with a Season One playoff trip the only thing keeping him off the floor.

The Basement

Bawce — Texas A&M, Frank — Washington, ZBO — SMU (a real 42-17 win over Lux's Cal this stretch, the rare bright spot down here), Gooch — Indiana, Bhog — BYU, Cozy — Baylor, Teeric — USC (back-to-back blowout losses, 61-14 to Penn State and 56-21 to Notre Dame), TV — UCLA, Thrash — South Carolina, and Quan — Florida State are all still hunting the signature result that moves them out of this tier. Nothing this stretch changed that math for any of them.

The Debut

JT — Nebraska and Fields — Auburn hold at the bottom, still finding their footing in programs they didn't build. They're joined by Lux — Cal, who took over a program that had been CPU-held since Quan left it in Season Two and opened his tenure with a real 42-17 loss to SMU. Newcomers start at the bottom of this board no matter who they are. Proving it is still the only way up.

That's the board after Tampa. The belt race is 2-1 Stew, and so is the top of this board. Pat has never been closer. Nobody has. Season Four decides whether the coin flip lands.
